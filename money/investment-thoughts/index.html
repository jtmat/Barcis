Investment Thoughts

Simple, diversified, and understandable is often more durable than clever.

Published July 23, 2026

Investing works best when the plan is clear enough to follow during both exciting markets and frightening ones.

My preference is to own broad groups of companies, keep costs low, and choose a mix of stocks, bonds, and cash that makes it possible to stay invested. The right allocation is not the one with the best theoretical return. It is the one you can live with.

Build around behavior

Markets will eventually test every plan. A slightly more conservative portfolio that you keep may serve you better than an aggressive one you abandon after a painful decline.

The goal is not constant activity. A good investment plan should require very few decisions once it is in place.

Keep the explanation short

If you cannot explain what you own and why in a few paragraphs, the portfolio may be too complicated. Complexity has a cost even when it does not appear on a statement.
