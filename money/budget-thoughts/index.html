Budget Thoughts

A budget should make life easier to understand—not turn every purchase into a moral decision.

Published July 23, 2026

The purpose of a budget is not to tell you that you cannot spend money. It is to show you what your money can do.

A useful budget begins with the life you actually live. Start with the fixed costs that keep that life running, then make room for the things you value: travel, comfort, generosity, hobbies, or simply a larger margin of safety.

Know the large numbers first

There is little value in tracking every cup of coffee while ignoring housing, transportation, taxes, insurance, and recurring subscriptions. Get the large categories right before worrying about small variations.

A useful rule: organize the budget well enough to make decisions, but not so finely that maintaining it becomes another job.

Leave room for real life

A plan that assumes every month will be perfect is not a plan. Repairs, medical costs, family needs, and opportunities will appear. A flexible category for irregular expenses keeps those events from feeling like failures.
